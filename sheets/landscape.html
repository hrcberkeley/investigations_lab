Introduction

In the Data Discovery Framework worksheet, we discussed at length how understanding where your data may live helps you figure out how to find it. This worksheet helps you operationalize the theory into a digital landscape assessment. The goal is not necessarily to already find information, but rather doing an exercise in imagination that will help you structure your search. In that sense, you should try to leverage your knowledge about digital data and the places where it usually lives. Trust your investigative sixth sense!

But before we begin, a nagging theoretical reminder: every document, post, image, or dataset is embedded in a web of relationships between producers, platforms, formats, languages, and communities. Your goal is to identify these relationships to create a discovery strategy.

The worksheet is designed for collaborative use. Work through it with your team, share your findings, and build on each other's contextual knowledge. Different team members will bring different expertise: one person may know the platforms, another the language, another the institutional landscape. You’re encouraged to (critically) leverage your own perspectives and worldviews to strengthen the collective effort!

5. Language and Vernacular

Since the web is an inherently communicative technology, digital data will always be embedded in language: national languages, slang, platform-specific terms, and community vernaculars. Using these variables to your advantage can help you to track the data faster and adopt specific viewpoints when needed. For example, the language of victims will be different to that of the authorities processing their testimony, and official documents tend to be ridden with bureaucratic terms that are missing in social media posts.

5.3 Hashtags and Keywords

6. Time: Temporal Strategy

Given the overabundance of digital data online, it’s never a bad idea to narrow the scope of your search to avoid drowning in information. Time is one of the most straightforward factors you can employ for reducing the amount of data you need to explore.
